Working memory and fluid intelligence

Working memory helps you hold and update information while using it. Fluid intelligence concerns reasoning through new problems. They are related, distinct abilities. Dual n-back challenges you to update two sequences while ignoring tempting matches at the wrong distance.

Fluid uses adaptive dual n-back as a repeatable exercise. Its interface, optional lessons and daily guide are product design choices; the app is not itself a published clinical intervention or an IQ test.

The encouraging early evidence

Jaeggi and colleagues reported improvements on fluid-intelligence measures following adaptive dual n-back training, with larger gains associated with more training. This PNAS study helped make the exercise widely known.

Jaeggi et al. (2008), Improving fluid intelligence with training on working memory ↗

A later meta-analysis by Au and colleagues, pooling twenty studies, reported a small positive effect on fluid-intelligence test outcomes. These findings are a reason to investigate the exercise; they do not establish that every person or every app gets the same effect.

Au et al. (2015), Improving fluid intelligence with training on working memory: a meta-analysis ↗

Why transfer matters

Getting better at n-back is different from getting better at unrelated tasks. A placebo-controlled study by Redick and colleagues found substantial improvement on the trained task without an improvement in fluid intelligence. Soveri and colleagues later analysed thirty-three randomized trials and found that much of the transfer was specific to n-back tasks.

Redick et al. (2013), randomized placebo-controlled study ↗

Soveri et al. (2017), Working memory training revisited ↗

The wider brain-training context

A large study published in Nature tested a broader set of computerized brain-training tasks and found improvement on trained tasks without general transfer. It was not a trial of Fluid or the same dual n-back protocol. It is useful context for reading broad claims about brain training.

Owen et al. (2010), Putting brain training to the test ↗

Publication in a journal does not imply that the journal or the researchers endorse Fluid.

What Fluid promises

A beautiful, focused way to practise dual n-back: adaptive rounds, optional learning, clear scores and a record of your exercise performance. Wider intelligence gains remain an active and contested research question. We do not promise an IQ increase or treatment of any condition.

Start gently, pay attention to how you feel, and use the references to explore the question for yourself.
