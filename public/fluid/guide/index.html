Begin with 1-back

A circle appears in one of eight places, and a recorded letter plays. At 1-back, compare each new position and sound with the immediately previous step. Press Position if the place matches. Press Sound if the letter matches.

If both match, press both. If neither matches, let the step pass. The two streams are independent: a position match does not mean the sound matches too.

What does n mean?

n is the number of steps to look back. At 2-back, compare with two steps earlier, not the last step. In the sound sequence C → G → C, the final C is a 2-back match. In C → C → G, the second C is a 1-back match, not a 2-back match.

The first n steps build the memory window. Notice them without expecting a scored match yet. A letter or position that feels familiar from the wrong distance is not a match.

Respond to the match

Use the on-screen Position and Sound controls. On desktop, the app also displays the relevant keyboard shortcuts. A selected control becomes clearly highlighted. You can select both for the same step.

Use a comfortable audio level. Settings lets you preview the letter recordings and adjust background music. No microphone input is involved.

Learn without pressure

Open Learn to choose a lesson: a single stream, a new memory depth, or bringing position and sound together. Return to any lesson whenever you want. The short 9-beat lesson practice stays separate from Classic history.

There is no compulsory pathway or onboarding lock. If you already know the exercise, go straight to Classic.

Classic finds your level

Classic begins at n = 1 and adapts after each completed round. Position and sound are scored separately. Both scores at least 90% move n up; either below 75% moves it down; otherwise n stays the same. Depth stays between 1 and 14.

Correctly leaving a non-match unpressed counts too. The result screen shows fixed threshold markers so you can see why the next depth was chosen. A lower n is an adjustment to the exercise, not a failure.

Build a sustainable practice

Fluid suggests twelve rounds as a daily guide. It is not a required minimum, a medical prescription or a guarantee of a particular benefit. Fewer is fine. Stop when you have had enough, and return when you want.

Progress shows completed rounds and day-by-day graphs. Use them to understand your practice, rather than treating an n score as a measure of your intelligence.
